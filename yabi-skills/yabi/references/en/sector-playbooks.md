# Sector Playbooks

This file gives direction, not templates.

## Barber / Salon

Core feeling: craft, sharpness, ritual, trust.

World:
- Metal, leather, mirror, hair texture, warm/cold light contrast.
- Section transitions that cut like blades.
- Booking flow must be immediate and clear.

Avoid:
- Salon photo plus pricing cards plus review cards.
- Forcing every service into identical boxes.

## Restaurant / Cafe

Core feeling: appetite, tempo, place, warmth.

World:
- Ingredient close-ups, service rhythm, tableside atmosphere.
- Menu pages should feel like discovery, not a static list.
- Reservation CTA should be warm but clear.

Avoid:
- Darkened food photo with unreadable white headline.
- Turning the menu into a card cemetery.

## Consulting / Agency

Core feeling: clarity, decision, trust, intelligence.

World:
- Maps, tables, decision points, documents, screens, data traces.
- Case study routes must carry real proof.
- Copy readability is non-negotiable.

Avoid:
- Abstract gradients and words like growth, strategy, innovation without
  evidence.
- Fake stock people when real team material is absent.

## SaaS / B2B Product

Core feeling: control, speed, system, reliability.

World:
- Real product UI must be a first-viewport signal.
- Dashboards should be readable and believable.
- Pricing, docs, and integrations need their own route logic.

Avoid:
- Talking around the product without showing it.
- Unreal glossy dashboard mockups.

## Portfolio / Creative Studio

Core feeling: signature, risk, proof, selectivity.

World:
- Work should feel like a rhythmic gallery, not a grid.
- Case studies should stage process and outcome separately.
- Navigation can become part of the gallery dramaturgy.

Avoid:
- Locking every project into identical thumbnail cards.
- Leaving "Selected Work" as a generic section.

## E-Commerce / Product

Core feeling: desire, detail, trust, decision.

World:
- Product must show scale, material, and use context.
- PDP, collection, cart, and checkout UI must share the same quality.
- Micro-interactions should reduce purchase friction.

Avoid:
- Turning the product into a decorative object.
- Sacrificing checkout readability for spectacle.
